Boondock Math

The gray tank ends more trips than the weather, the battery's name counts amp-hours it will never give, and the furnace counts its nights in bottles.

The tank truth

At the 4-gallon navy-shower norm (labeled), every person aboard fills the gray tank a little each day. The 40-gallon tank with two people is a working week at most - and it ends the weekend early at four.

The battery truth

Lead-acid honestly gives half its amp-hours, lithium about 80% (labeled norms). What is left, divided by the day's draw, is the nights the lights actually last.

The bottle truth

A 20-pound bottle holds about 12 honest furnace-hours (labeled norm). Divide by the hours the furnace runs each night and the bottles name the cold nights left.

What it never does

It never counts the amp-hours the chemistry keeps, never reads the brochure's week as your week, and never forgets the norms are labeled - the dump station keeps the honest ledger.

Boondock Math is open source - the engine is small enough to read, and the math is plain enough to argue with.
